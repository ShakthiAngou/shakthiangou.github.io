Veganism and Supplementation

I believed that a nutritionally adequate vegan diet should not require vitamin supplementation.

As a life-long vegetarian and vegan of several years now, in the early days of my vegan journey, I was in denial about the efficacy of supplemental vitamins. The philosophical driver behind my switch to veganism is rooted in the belief that humans are evolutionarily built to adapt to the food available, and hence are not inherently tied to meat, and that the status quo of factory farming is simply unethical and unsustainable. I felt that, for this belief to hold its ground, a vegan diet should be able to sustain itself entirely through whole foods. Supplementation almost felt like conceding that the diet was inherently deficient, and I wanted to prove the opposite.

What changed my mind was realising that this standard was based on an overly idealised view of how we obtain food in the first place. Modern agriculture, including plant agriculture, has deviated substantially from natural processes of growth, and modern diets of all kinds routinely rely on fortified foods and supplementation to address nutritional gaps. I no longer see supplementation as evidence against the viability of veganism, but simply as a practical tool for meeting nutritional needs in a modern food system.
